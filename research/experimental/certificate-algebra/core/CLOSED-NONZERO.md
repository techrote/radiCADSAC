## Theorem E2 — exact necessary and sufficient endpoint refinement

For a Bernstein polynomial p(u) = sum b_i B_i^n(u) on 0<=u<=1, assume its independently verified coefficient vector is in a *nonzero weak-sign cone*: all b_i>=0, at least one >0, or all b_i<=0, at least one <0.

1. In the positive cone, p(u)>0 for every 0<u<1. It is strictly positive on the **entire closed cell** if and only if b_0>0 **and** b_n>0.
2. In the negative cone, p(u)<0 for every 0<u<1. It is strictly negative on the **entire closed cell** if and only if b_0<0 **and** b_n<0.
3. For a C0 finite-piece source already verified by B1, closed-domain nonvanishing is equivalent to nonzero sign-cone status on every piece and nonzero Bernstein endpoint coefficients on **both ends of each piece**. Seam continuity then forces a single global sign; identity-zero leaves and zero knots are refused. B1 UNKNOWN/mixed-sign leaves remain unresolved, even if a different proof might establish their positivity.

**Proof:** Every Bernstein basis function B_i^n is strictly positive in (0,1). Hence any nonzero weak-sign vector yields the claimed strict interior sign. At u=0, p(0)=b_0; at u=1, p(1)=b_n. This makes the endpoint conditions necessary and sufficient, independently of how close another zero might be. The finite C0-piece conjunction is sufficient and necessary for the entire closed domain, and C0 forbids a sign change between two adjacent strictly nonzero endpoints. This is classical Bernstein mathematics, not a novel positivity theorem. The law covers an **infinite rational-amplitude family**, subject only to the finite implementation degree/bit limits.

**Less conservative than all-coefficients-positive:** the degree-two Bernstein vector (1,0,1) represents p(u)=(1-u)^2+u^2, strictly positive on [0,1]. The internal zero coefficient defeats A1's sufficient all-coefficients-positive test, but E2 certifies closed positivity. Similarly (1,0,...,0,1) is strictly positive for all degrees n>=2 (software caps n at 24); there are infinitely many such rational polynomial sources under rational affine domains/amplitudes.

**Necessary B5 falsifier:** adjacent C0 pieces p_L(u)=1-u and p_R(u)=u are both B1 POS on their *open* cells but vanish at their common knot. E2 MUST refuse both pieces. If multiplied into [G(t)]^2 for G(t)=sin(pi*t)-t cos(pi*t) with the knot at k+1/2 for integer k>=3, the product gains a zero absent in G.
